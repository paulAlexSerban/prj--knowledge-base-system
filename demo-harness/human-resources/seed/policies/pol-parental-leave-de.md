# Parental Leave Policy (Germany)

## Eligibility

Every employee in Germany is eligible from their first day of employment. The policy covers birth parents, fathers and second parents, and adoptive parents or foster parents. Part-time employees are covered on the same terms.

## Statutory leave (Elternzeit)

German law gives each parent up to three years of unpaid parental leave (Elternzeit) per child. Up to 24 months of this entitlement can be taken between the child's third and eighth birthday with the employer's agreement. Employment is protected from the moment you give notice, at most eight weeks before the leave starts for a child under three, until the leave ends.

## Company pay

The company adds to the statutory parental allowance (Elterngeld):

- The birth parent receives 16 weeks of full salary, starting at the beginning of the statutory maternity protection period.
- The second parent receives 8 weeks of full salary, which can be taken in one block or in two blocks within the first 12 months.
- Adoptive and foster parents receive 12 weeks of full salary from the placement date.

Statutory Elterngeld is paid by the state at 65 to 67 percent of net income, up to 1,800 euros per month, for up to 14 months when shared between parents. The company top-up brings the weeks listed above to full net pay. It does not extend the statutory period.

## Part-time work during leave

You may work up to 32 hours a week during Elternzeit. Tell your manager and the People team at least seven weeks before you want to start. We will confirm in writing within four weeks.

## How to apply

1. Send written notice to your manager and to people@example.com, with the expected birth date and the dates you want to take.
2. The People team confirms the dates and the company top-up in writing.
3. Submit the Elterngeld application to the local Elterngeldstelle. The People team provides the employer certificate within five working days.

## Returning to work

You return to the same role or an equivalent one in the same team and location. A return-to-work conversation takes place two weeks before your return date.
